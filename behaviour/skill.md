# Behavior Interview Scenario Skill

Use this skill to collect scenarios that cover every behavioral question below. Ask for one scenario at a time and keep the answers focused on real examples using the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

## Goal

Convert a list of behavioral interview questions into a set of strong, complete scenarios that cover:
- conflict and disagreement
- ownership and helping teammates
- failure and learning
- deadlines and pressure
- decision-making with limited information
- innovation and initiative
- teamwork and trust
- communication and stakeholder management

## How the skill works

1. Start with the user by asking them to pick a scenario for the first behavioral question.
2. Ask for exactly one example at a time.
3. For each answer, probe for:
   - What was the situation?
   - What was your role and responsibility?
   - What did you do?
   - What was the outcome?
   - What did you learn?
4. Map each answer to one or more behavior categories.
5. Stop only when all question areas are covered or the user says they are done.

## Prompt template

Use this prompt when interacting with the user:

"I’ll help you prepare for a behavioral round interview. I’ll ask you for one scenario at a time to cover all the behaviors below. For each scenario, answer in STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, and Result. If you are unsure, I’ll help you select the best real example from your experience."

## Interviewer follow-up questions

After the user gives a scenario, ask these follow-ups to make it strong:

- What was the situation?
- What was your exact responsibility?
- What were the constraints or challenges?
- What options did you consider?
- Why did you choose that approach?
- What did you personally do?
- What was the result?
- What did you learn?
- Would you do anything differently now?

## Final checklist

Before ending the session, confirm the user has coverage for:
- conflict
- teamwork and trust
- failure/mistake
- deadlines
- helping others
- ownership and initiative
- decision-making without data
- innovation
- growth and feedback
- tough trade-offs

## Example final instruction

"We’ve covered all the core behavioral areas. Let’s now refine each scenario for clarity, brevity, and impact so your answers sound confident and structured."
